Design a polished, high-fidelity, responsive platform called Visada. Visada is a white-label incident reporting and residential-life support platform for universities. For this pilot, configure and brand the platform for the University of California, Santa Barbara.

The campus-facing product should be titled UCSB Residential Life Reporting, with the attribution Powered by Visada shown subtly underneath. UCSB should be the primary brand throughout the Resident Assistant and administrator experiences. Visada branding should appear only in places such as the login footer, help and support area, privacy and legal footer, system administration settings, and Powered by Visada attribution.

Build the product using a reusable white-label design system so another university can replace UCSB branding without redesigning the interface. Make the institution name, institution logo, campus colors, typography, login messaging, support contacts, residence-hall terminology, incident-report terminology, severity labels, report templates, privacy notices, wellness contacts, administrator role names, SSO provider, and footer content configurable. Do not hard-code UCSB branding into reusable components.

The initial pilot is for UCSB Residential Life and includes two connected experiences. The first is a mobile-first web experience for UCSB Resident Assistants to document and submit incidents. The second is a desktop administration dashboard for authorized UCSB Residential Life staff to review reports, understand resident history, manage wellness-check reminders, review audit activity, and create authorized exports.

The pilot should support mobile voice, text, and photo capture; AI-generated structured incident reports; AI severity suggestions that the RA must confirm; a post-submission RA wellness check-in; The Ledger for lower-level floor observations; a severity-ranked administrator queue; resident past-history charts; an AI summary displayed beside the full report; threshold-based wellness-check reminders; a minimum resident pattern and query layer; FERPA-aware data handling; role-based access control; audit logging; encryption; UCSB SSO through Shibboleth; and SOC 2 readiness controls.

Do not include the Phase 2 front-desk log system, the full RA burnout analytics dashboard, summer-camp functionality, or senior-living functionality.

Create a UCSB-branded institutional login page with a UCSB logo placeholder, the page title UCSB Residential Life Reporting, and the supporting text Secure incident documentation and residential-life support. The main action should be Sign in with UCSB NetID using Shibboleth or campus SSO. Include a FERPA and privacy notice, a campus support link, a secure-session indicator, and subtle Powered by Visada attribution in the footer.

Do not create a separate Visada username and password flow for regular UCSB users. Include a less-prominent emergency administrator login option for authorized break-glass access during an SSO outage.

For the RA mobile experience, use the navigation labels Home, New Report, The Ledger, My Reports, Wellness, and Help.

For the UCSB administrator experience, use the navigation labels Overview, Report Queue, Residents, The Ledger, Wellness Checks, Analytics, Legal Exports, Audit Log, and Administration.

Use Resident rather than Student in operational navigation where appropriate. Continue using the term student education record in FERPA notices and compliance content.

Use realistic but fictional UCSB sample data. Example residence halls and locations can include Santa Rosa Hall, Anacapa Hall, San Miguel Hall, Residence Hall A, Building 4 Third Floor, Community Lounge, and East Courtyard. Do not use real student identities.

Example roles should include Resident Assistant, Resident Director, Assistant Resident Director, Area Coordinator, Residential Life Director, RA Support Coordinator, UCSB System Administrator, and Legal or Compliance Reviewer.

Example report categories should include Noise Concern, Alcohol-Related Incident, Property Damage, Resident Conflict, Medical Concern, Community Policy Concern, Welfare Concern, and Other.

The UCSB pilot should feel calm, credible, campus-oriented, supportive, secure, easy to use during late-night incidents, and appropriate for sensitive education records. Avoid making the interface feel punitive, clinical, or similar to a police system.

Use UCSB branding as the main visual layer while keeping Visada’s reusable component system underneath. Use a university-style blue as the primary interface color, a restrained gold accent, white and light-neutral surfaces, and clear dark text. Reserve red and amber for severity and urgent warnings. Use green only for confirmed completion or successful actions.

Use high-contrast typography, accessible severity labels, icons alongside color, moderate corner rounding, clear spacing, strong visual hierarchy, and a modern sans-serif typeface. Severity should never be communicated through color alone.

Create branding through reusable design variables for institution name, institution short name, institution logo, primary brand color, secondary brand color, support email, support phone, privacy link, SSO button label, and Powered by brand attribution.

Include a small administration preview showing how the UCSB logo, colors, terminology, contact details, and SSO label could be replaced for another university without changing the underlying interface.

Use the footer text UCSB Residential Life Reporting followed by Securely powered by Visada. Include Privacy, FERPA Notice, Accessibility, Support, and Security links.
